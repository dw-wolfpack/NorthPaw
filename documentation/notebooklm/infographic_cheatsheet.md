## 🛣️ Infographic 1: Pavement Heat Threat (Surface Multipliers)

### 📋 Key Content Bullets
*   **Pavement is Not Air Temp**: Pavement absorbs solar radiation. At an air temperature of 87°F, black asphalt can easily reach a dangerous **140°F**, which can cause second-degree burns on canine paws in under 60 seconds (JAMA Dermatology standards).
*   **Surface Heat Multipliers**: Pavement materials retain heat differently based on albedo and conductivity:
    *   *Artificial Turf*: **1.38x** (rubber infill acts as a heat trap)
    *   *Sand*: **1.15x** (low conductivity, high surface heat)
    *   *Asphalt*: **1.0x** (baseline)
    *   *Cobblestone*: **0.85x** (engineering thermal estimate)
    *   *Concrete*: **0.72x** (higher reflectivity)
*   **Canine Safety Bands**: Paw safety is graded by pavement heat:
    *   *Safe (< 77°F)*: Zero risk.
    *   *Warm (77°F – 105°F)*: Safe for normal walks.
    *   *Hot (105°F – 125°F)*: Monitor for discomfort.
    *   *Danger (>= 125°F)*: Burn hazard. Walk on grass or use paw protection.

### 🎨 Style & Layout Settings
*   **Choose Visual Style**: `Scientific`
*   **Choose Orientation**: `Portrait`
*   **Level of Detail**: `Standard` or `Detailed`

Create a Scientific style infographic in Portrait orientation showing pavement safety temperatures for dogs. ## 🐕 Infographic 2: Canine Biology & NPI Calibration

### 📋 Key Content Bullets
*   **Snout Profile Offset**: Flat-faced (brachycephalic) breeds like Pugs, Bulldogs, or Boxers have a **+15% risk multiplier** applied to their safety index because their compressed airways make cooling down through panting much less efficient.
*   **Coat & Color Retention**: Double-coated dogs (Huskies, Golden Retrievers) receive a **+10% heat retention risk factor**. Additionally, dark-colored coats absorb solar energy much faster than lighter coats.
*   **Activity Baseline Adjustment**: High-energy, high-intensity dogs have their safest daily walk window durations **reduced by 20%** to prevent metabolic heat buildup and heat stress during exercise.

### 🎨 Style & Layout Settings
*   **Choose Visual Style**: `Bento Grid`
*   **Choose Orientation**: `Square`
*   **Level of Detail**: `Standard`

### ✍️ Generator Prompt

Use a warm, modern, high-contrast visual style to clearly separate the three inputs.

## 📝 Infographic 3: The 3-Step Pre-Walk Safety Checklist

### 📋 Key Content Bullets
*   **Step 1: Check Local Conditions**: Never look at air temperature alone. Check the Canine Heat Stress Index (CHSI) which combines air temperature and relative humidity to measure systemic heat stress.
*   **Step 2: Run the 7-Second Hand Test**: Before letting your dog walk, place the back of your hand flat against the pavement. If you cannot comfortably hold it there for 7 seconds, it is too hot for your dog's paws.
*   **Step 3: Pack & Plan the Outing**: Check the NPI dial for optimal walk windows. Pack clean water for hydration, a cooling towel, and paw protection boots if walking on hot surfaces is unavoidable.

### 🎨 Style & Layout Settings
*   **Choose Visual Style**: `Sketch Note` or `Instructional`
*   **Choose Orientation**: `Portrait`
*   **Level of Detail**: `Standard` or `Concise`

Create an Instructional style infographic in Portrait orientation showing a simple 3-Step Pre-Walk Safety Checklist. Highlight Step 1: Check Local Conditions (combining temperature and humidity), Step 2: Run the 7-Second Hand Test (if it is too hot for your hand for 7 seconds, it is too hot for paws), and Step 3: Pack and Plan (NPI windows, water, and paw boots). Use a clean sketch-note format with friendly green, amber, and red safety accent colors.
